Thesis · Chapter 4 Findings · plan

Teasing out the “inside-out” finding

Chapter 5 asserts the study’s central claim. Chapter 4 never shows it. This is the plan to make the Findings demonstrate — through matched cross-case comparison — that comparable use cases diverged because of organizational conditions, not the model.

The claim to be evidenced Similar use cases — a content-generation pipeline, an analytics agent, customer-service assistance — produced different outcomes across organizations. That variance traced to organizational conditions, managerial behavior, and the configuration in which the technology was applied, rather than to the underlying model.

01

Why Chapter 4 doesn’t land it

The claim has two halves — same use case → different outcomes (the variance) and the difference traces to conditions, not the model (the explanation). The current structure establishes neither.

Reason 1

A thematic mosaic

4.3.1 and 4.4 introduce each theme with a single interviewee. That architecture cannot show variance — variance needs the same use case shown across two orgs, side by side.

Reason 2

Asserted, then abandoned

The 4.4 intro states the “inside-out” finding outright, then the subsections revert to listing benefits, sacrifices, and risks generically. The reader is told the finding, never shown it.

Reason 3

The loop never closes

Figure 1’s chain (conditions → applying → outcomes) is the argument. But no passage connects a specific condition from 4.2 to a specific outcome divergence in 4.4.

04

How far the data lets us push it

This shapes the wording. The variance and the organizational explanation are solid; the sharper “it’s the harness, not the model” formulation rests on fewer voices — consistent with your own memo (“Only Eric talked about leveraging a harness”).

ROBUST
Organizational conditions & managerial behavior gate value
Many participants, often explicit: I9 “it’s like a pie … a whole operational model”; I13 “GenAI can’t solve any of your problems … if you dump it on a company with no clue, it will fail”; I12 “efficiency … everybody is gonna get the benefit”; I17 “three things align → a fantastic implementation … or it just stops”; I14 “30% technology, 60–70% humans/organization.”
SCOPED
“Configuration / harness, rather than the model”
Rests mainly on I5 (“who finds the harness … that’s gonna determine a lot of what our capabilities are”), with partial support from I16 (“add to cart — five definitions”) and I14 (fences beat generic models). Present as participant-attributed, not a settled cross-case fact.
THIN
A clean A-vs-B across two named orgs
Almost none exists; variance is assembled by the analyst from mostly one-sided accounts. The within-org cases (I11 standardized vs creative; I10 “after a million … the same output, just a new system”) are the closest to natural experiments. Several strong quotes are consultants generalizing, and I9 concedes “a lot of the stuff we talk about, we don’t actually do.”

06

Scoping the Chapter 5 claim

A small wording change keeps the claim honest about what the data carries.

As written
… that variance was attributable to organizational conditions, managerial behavior, and the configuration in which the technology was applied rather than to the underlying model.
Proposed
… that variance was attributable to organizational conditions, managerial behavior, and — where participants could articulate it — the configuration (the “harness”) in which the technology was applied, rather than to the underlying model.
