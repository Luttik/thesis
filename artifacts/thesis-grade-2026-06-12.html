Verdict

This is an 8-level piece of thinking inside a 6-level draft. The topic is genuinely novel and timely, the 17-interview grounded-theory base is solid, the findings tell a convincing quote-rich story, and the practical recommendations are concrete and well-weighted — exactly where the rubric puts 40% of the marks (Results + Practical). What holds it back is not the argument but the unfinished state of the manuscript: a whole Findings subsection (§4.5 Paradoxes) is promised in the contents and cited six times in the Discussion but is missing from the body, an editor's placeholder note is sitting in the Discussion text, section numbers don't line up, and a layer of citations is still flagged as unverified.

On the rubric's half-grade scale the content earns a 7.5–8; the execution defects pull it to a defensible 7 if handed in today. The good news is that almost every deduction below is mechanical — restore two sections, run the cross-references, fix the typos — rather than conceptual. Closing them is a few focused days, not a rewrite, and lands a clean 7.5 reaching toward 8. The Defense (10%) is a live event and is not assessed here.

What is working — protect these

  • The topic and its timing. Empirical, in-practice agentic AI in marketing is genuinely under-studied; you cite and directly answer the calls for research (Kim 2025; Mogaji & Jain 2024; Jain et al. 2024). That alone carries the innovativeness mark.
  • The findings narrative. Chapter 4 reads as a story, not a code dump: observing → steering → applying → value, each anchored in vivid, well-chosen quotes. This is precisely the "convincing account… tells a story" the 20% Results criterion asks for.
  • The practical chapter. Six implications that a CMO could act on Monday, plus the Appendix B checklist. Non-abstract, traceable to the data — the 20% practical weight is well earned.
  • The central model (Figure 1). A clean, legible process model that gives the thesis a spine and a memorable contribution.
  • The paradox lens. "The technology that creates the risk also supplies the means to manage it" (govern-AI-with-AI) is a sharp, original organizing idea — provided the section that develops it is actually restored (see below).
  • The prose mostly avoids the generic-LLM smell the rubric explicitly penalises — it has a voice. Keep it.

Blocking§4.5 "Paradoxes" is missing from the Findings body
The Table of Contents lists 4.5 Paradoxes (p.45) and the Discussion cites it repeatedly — 4.5.1, 4.5.2, 4.5.3, 4.5.4, and 4.4.4 — but the chapter jumps straight from 4.4.3 Risks to the Discussion. Blocking§4.1.3 "supplier communication" is also missing — same defect, second instance
Observing has only two sub-sections in the body (4.1.1 AI progression, 4.1.2 Market pressure), yet a third external condition — supplier / vendor communication — is treated as real everywhere else: it's a focused code in Table 3 ("Observing supplier communication"), it's named in the model description (§5.1, "progression of AI, market pressure and supplier communication"), and the Discussion points to Section 4.1.3 twice. The body needs the 4.1.3 section those references promise. Note the new Figure 1 dropped "Vendors" from external conditions, which deepens rather than fixes this gap.

Major — lifts the grade toward 8

MajorDeepen §2.3, the value framework
Value is the construct in your research question, yet §2.3 is the thinnest part of the review and leans on a practitioner HBR source (Almquist). Bring the value-co-creation lens (Vargo & Lusch; Orlikowski) forward from the Discussion into the framework so the "value created vs. value captured" and "value destruction" moves later are pre-loaded, not sprung on the reader. This is the cheapest way to raise both the Framework and Discussion marks.
MajorResolve the GenAI-vs-agentic framing in the Introduction
The RQ and contribution are about agentic AI, but the academic/practical contribution bullets repeatedly say "GenAI". Pick the agentic framing and make the bullets specific to this study's actual contribution (the process model, the harness, value-as-portfolio) rather than the current generic list.
MajorNew Figure 1 matches the headings — now align it with its own caption and contribution claim
improved The redrawn figure mirrors the Chapter 4 headings exactly — good, the earlier vocabulary clash is gone. But it now under-tells the thesis's own argument in two ways. Clear every highlight, confirm author/year/DOI against the actual papers, and check the ∼70 reference entries for consistency (DOIs present and formatted the same way throughout).
