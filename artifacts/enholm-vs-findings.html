Thesis · literature positioning

Enholm et al. (2022) vs. our findings

Enholm, Papagiannidis, Mikalef & Krogstie (2022), Artificial Intelligence and Business Value: A Literature Review (Information Systems Frontiers), is the paper your §2.4/§5.1.1 already lean on for the digital-paradox / complementary-assets claim. The headline: both studies locate value outside the technology — in the organizational resources and configuration around it — but they reach it from opposite ends (a firm-level synthesis of pre-GenAI AI vs. a grounded account of agentic AI in marketing).

Lens
Digital transformation + value co-creation; grounded, sensitizing concepts
Level
Managerial / process-oriented, interpretive (emic)
Technology
Agentic AI — LLM + memory + tools + autonomy, 2025
The shared core — why §5.1.1 cites Enholm

Enholm's central construct is the AI capability: AI-specific resources only create value when combined with complementary organizational resources; firms "struggle to realize value from AI" despite investment. That is the same claim as our central finding — value is produced by the configuration-in-use (the harness + conditions + managerial behavior), not the technology — and the same value gap our Table 4 makes visible. Enholm is the literature-side corroboration of your continuation argument.

Convergence and extension

Where we converge (corroboration)
  • Value is not in the technology. Enholm's complementary-resources / AI-capability thesis ≈ our configuration-in-use.
  • The value gap is real. "Firms struggle to realize value despite investment" ≈ our divergent outcomes for comparable use cases (the digital paradox).
  • Overlapping enablers. Data & infrastructure, leadership/top-management support, skills, competitive pressure, and governance/regulation appear in both.
  • Contingency, not one-best-way. Enholm flags contingency theory and value co-destruction; we develop both empirically as configuration and paradox.
Where we extend / diverge
  • The harness — we name the specific complementary asset for agentic AI (data access, memory, tools, guardrails, prompt, model), where Enholm stays generic.
  • Managerial micro-foundations — reshape/leverage/navigate + the AI champion, vs Enholm's firm-level, manager-implicit account.
  • Value as a portfolio with destruction + recursive paradox, vs Enholm's mostly positive first-/second-order effects.
  • Agentic & constructivist — agents that act (beyond automation/augmentation) and the contested "agentic" category itself — no analog in a pre-GenAI synthesis.
  • Efficiency as non-differentiating (competed away), a strategic nuance beyond "efficiency = positive effect."

How to use this in the thesis

Positioning
Enholm is your strongest literature-side anchor for §5.1.1's core move. Cite it where you claim value is mediated by complementary assets / organizational follow-through rather than the tool, and where you invoke the digital paradox — it shows the pre-GenAI AI-value literature already reached that conclusion, so your agentic findings continue rather than contradict it. Then mark the extension explicitly: the harness as the concrete complementary asset, the managerial repertoire / champion, and value-as-portfolio-managed-through-paradox are where the agentic, grounded, marketing account goes beyond Enholm's firm-level synthesis. One honest caveat to state: Enholm is a literature review of broadly-defined AI before generative models, so the convergence is at the level of the value-creation logic, not the specific technology.
Source read in full: Enholm, I. M., Papagiannidis, E., Mikalef, P., & Krogstie, J. (2022). Artificial Intelligence and Business Value: A Literature Review. Information Systems Frontiers, 24(5), 1709–1734 (online 2021). Compared against Chapter 4 (Findings) and §5.1 (Contributions) of the thesis.
