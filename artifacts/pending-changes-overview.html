Why I avoid editing on top of open changes

When a paragraph already has an unaccepted insertion, that text lives inside a tracked-change wrapper. Layering a second edit over it either nests changes (invalid) or produces overlapping insert/delete marks that render as a tangle in the Review pane. Cleaner to let you accept/reject first, then edit — so the diff stays readable.

How to clear the deck

Filter by author in the Review pane.

Word → Review → Tracked Changes → Show Markup → Specific People. Handle one author at a time so my edits and your supervisor's don't blur together.

For my edits, accept insert + delete together.

Each reword is a matched pair (new text inserted, old text struck). Accepting both gives the final wording. The only exception is the §5.1 [315] orphan deletion — accept it on its own.

Review your supervisor's Ch 1–2 edits separately.

Those reflect her intent, not mine — read them on their own terms.

Then ping me for the next pass.

Once a section is accepted, I can edit it cleanly without stacking changes — which is exactly the situation that made me route around [322]/[335] this time.
