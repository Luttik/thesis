The reframe

Both sections are currently organized around mapping the empirical model onto dynamic-capabilities microfoundations (observing→sensing, steering→seizing, applying→reconfiguring). That scaffolding is not the point of the research. We keep dynamic capabilities as the background lens but lead with the substantive argument.

Move away from
  • Walking through sensing / seizing / transforming as the structure
  • "Observing corresponds to sensing; steering to seizing…" term-by-term mapping
  • Framing the contribution as "we extend the DC framework"
Center on
  • What earlier digital transformations teach (organizational, not technological; the digital paradox)
  • How that lesson applies to agentic AI (value lives in the configuration / harness)

The analytical core: how agentic AI differs

This is the spine that replaces the mapping. §2.4 raises it as the open question the lessons leave; §5.1.1 answers it from the findings. Three candidate differences — I'd feature the first two and keep the third light.

Where advantage comes from
Sensing opportunities and threats is itself a differentiator (the dynamic-capabilities emphasis).
Sensing is cheap and shared — everyone sees the same hockey-stick, pressure, and roadmaps. The binding constraint moves to follow-through and orchestration (Table 4).
Role of the technology
A passive instrument; humans operate it, and complementary assets (skills, process, structure) sit around it.
An actor that performs the work; the decisive complementary asset moves inward to the harness — data, memory, tools, guardrails, prompt, model.

Proposed §2.4 — Learnings from earlier waves of digital transformation

Two tight paragraphs: the lesson, then the lens + the open question. Keeps both notes you like; trims the microfoundations walk-through.

¶1 · the lesson
Digital transformation is organizational, not technological — it changes processes, business models, capabilities, and sometimes identity. And its value is mediated by complementary assets, which is why investment so often disappoints (the digital paradox): a technically capable agent creates no value on its own; it must be embedded in a work system.
Verhoef 2021 · Wessel 2021 · Hanelt 2021 · Ancillai 2023 · Enholm 2022 · Teece 1986
¶2 · the lens + the question
Dynamic capabilities (the firm's capacity to renew its resource base under change) is the lens for this, and has already been carried into digital transformation. But agentic AI may differ from earlier waves in ways that bear directly on value creation — the question Chapter 5 takes up.
Teece 1997 · Teece 2007 · Warner & Wäger 2019 · Ellström 2021 · Hanelt 2021
keep: DT-as-org-change keep: digital paradox + complementary assets trim: sensing/seizing/transforming walk-through → one clause trim: digitization/digitalization definitions (or shorten) add: one sentence raising "how agentic AI differs"

Proposed §5.1.1 — Extending dynamic capabilities to agentic AI in marketing

Two paragraphs, structured as the two differences — no term-by-term mapping. The contribution becomes "here is how agentic AI departs from earlier digital transformation," answered from the data.

¶1 · lesson applies + difference #1
The digital-transformation lesson holds and sharpens: value is produced by the configuration around the agentic technology, not the technology itself — organizational, even at the level of a single use case. But agentic AI departs from earlier waves in where the constraint sits: sensing was not scarce (everyone saw the same frontier), so differentiation relocated to organizational follow-through and orchestration (Section 4.4.4; Table 4).
Verhoef/Wessel/Hanelt · Ancillai 2023 · Enholm 2022 · Holmström 2022
¶2 · difference #2 + the bridge + handoff
A second departure: the agent is an actor, not an instrument, so the complementary assets that mediate value take a new form — the harness and the conditions around the model. This gives concrete content to asset orchestration, the step dynamic capabilities names but leaves thin. How that configuration co-produces value is developed in §5.1.2; whether such advantages can be captured rather than only created is left open.
Teece 2007 · Teece 1986 · Lepak 2007 · (links to §5.1.2)
