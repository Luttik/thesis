Overall assessment

This is a strong, well-architected Findings chapter. It has a genuine analytic spine — the Observe → Navigate → Apply → Obtain-value chain — that maps onto your theoretical coding (Table 3) and reads as a process model rather than a quote dump. Your best moments hold two divergent cases side by side and let the contrast carry an argument.

The weaknesses are mostly executional and structural, not conceptual: broken section numbering, dangling cross-references, a promised category that never appears, a duplicated passage, and a few places where the framing the chapter promises doesn't match what it delivers. None are fatal; all are fixable. A handful of conceptual tensions are worth sharpening before an examiner finds them.

Conceptual — sharpen before submission
C6
"Conditions" vs "behaviours" are blurred in §4.2
sharpen

§4.2 The verb-titles (Steering, Leveraging, Dealing with) promise manager agency, but much of the content describes conditions/barriers outside the manager's control — §4.2.3 even concedes "the data shows no clear patterns of reshaping behavior."

Table 3 actually separates conditions (capacity, technical resources, governance) from navigating responses (reshaping, leveraging, navigating constraints), but the chapter collapses them — so Table 3 and the §4.2 subsection structure can't be reconciled by a reader. Name which parts are conditions the manager reads and which are behaviours the manager enacts.

C7
Your strongest cross-cutting finding is never stated as one
sharpen

§4.3 The recurring insight — the same model/tool produces opposite value depending on data integration and context — is the analytic payoff of §4.3 and the whole point of Table 4, but it only appears implicitly inside examples.

Fix: state it explicitly as a topic sentence early in §4.3. That single move lifts §4.3.1–4.3.4 from a use-case catalogue into an argument.
C8
The sacrifices/risks taxonomy is inconsistent for job displacement
sharpen

§4.4.2 You define sacrifices as negative outcomes "highly likely to materialize" and risks as "unlikely or highly unknown." Yet job replacement is filed under sacrifices, while the evidence says only one interviewee saw actual job loss and I13 says "it is not the way companies are doing it right now."

Fix: by your own definitions that reads like a risk. Either move it, or reframe the evidence to show it materialising.
C9
The compliance section contradicts itself
sharpen

§4.2.4 The section asserts managers "accept the compliance frameworks as they are" and "no attempts to alter or negotiate … were observed" — then two paragraphs later describes work-arounds, shadow IT, and ignoring policies "altogether."

Fix: reconcile — they don't negotiate upward, but they actively route around. Circumvention is navigating the constraint, and that is a more interesting claim.
C10
"Mediates" is a strong causal word; Figure 1 is never walked through
sharpen

§4 intro The chapter says managerial behaviour "mediates value creation." For a qualitative, non-causal design, consider "appears to mediate" / "is associated with."

Figure 1 is introduced at the top but never referenced again or explained — define what its arrows mean (sequence? mechanism?) and tie each section back to it.

C11
Evidence is concentrated in a few voices
sharpen

whole chapter Citation counts within Chapter 4: I13 ≈ 16, I6 ≈ 15, I12 ≈ 13, I10 ≈ 11 — while I5 appears twice and Interviewee 17 (of N = 17) is never cited at all. This is a known GT risk: themes driven by a few articulate participants.

Fix: add a sentence acknowledging it, and either cite I17 somewhere or note that later interviews densified existing categories — which also substantiates your saturation claim.
