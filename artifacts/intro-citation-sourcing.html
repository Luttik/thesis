Citation 1 — attention paid to (Gen)AI

01

“Significant attention to GenAI in academic literature and practitioner discourse”

Stefanie B. “this needs to be cited.”

Despite the significant attention paid to GenAI in both academic literature and practitioner discourse (Chintalapati & Pandey, 2022), a critical gap exists in understanding how marketing managers can systematically leverage GenAI to create value beyond content generation.

Citation added

Chintalapati, S., & Pandey, S. K. (2022). Artificial intelligence in marketing: A systematic literature review. International Journal of Market Research, 64(1), 38–68. — filed locally as “chintalapati & shivendra - 2021”; same paper, online-first 2021.

In the paper
Academician implications · p. ~19 covers both halves
At this point, the research on AI in marketing is heavily dominated by consultants and practitioners, although the topic has witnessed growing attention from academics in the past couple of years.
Introduction · p. 2
…the topic of AI-powered marketing has been increasing in relevance and attracting growing attention among the world’s researchers.
Abstract · p. 1
The study discusses the practitioner and academic research implications and proposes a future research agenda…
⚠ Fit note
This paper is about AI in marketing generally and was written before the GenAI / ChatGPT wave. It solidly supports “significant attention to AI across academic and practitioner spheres,” but it does not speak to generative AI specifically. If Stefanie wanted backing for GenAI’s prominence in particular, this cite sits slightly upstream of the claim.

Citation 2 — the digital-transformation gap

02

“Existing DT literature doesn’t fully account for GenAI / agentic systems”

Stefanie B. “this needs to be cited.”

Furthermore, the existing literature on digital transformation, while valuable, does not fully account for the unique characteristics of GenAI and agentic systems (Hanelt et al., 2021; Verhoef et al., 2021).

Citations added

Hanelt, A., Bohnsack, R., Marz, D., & Antunes Marante, C. (2021). A systematic review of the literature on digital transformation: Insights and implications for strategy and organizational change. Journal of Management Studies, 58(5), 1159–1197.

Verhoef, P. C., Broekhuizen, T., Bart, Y., Bhattacharya, A., Qi Dong, J., Fabian, N., & Haenlein, M. (2021). Digital transformation: A multidisciplinary reflection and research agenda. Journal of Business Research, 122, 889–901.

In Hanelt et al. (2021) — abstract, p. 1159
Abstract scopes the field + names the gap
…we find that the nature of DT is only partially covered by conventional frameworks on organizational change. On the basis of this analysis, we derive a research agenda…
Abstract
…a systematic review of the extensive yet diverse and fragmented literature on digital transformation (DT)… On the basis of 279 articles, we provide a multi-dimensional framework…
In Verhoef et al. (2021) — p. 889
Definition
…a change in how a firm employs digital technologies, to develop a new digital business model that helps to create and appropriate more value for the firm.
Three-stage framework & drivers
…digitization, digitalization, and digital transformation… [organizations] invest heavily in digital agility, networking capability, and big data analytics.
⚠ Fit note
Neither paper literally says “DT theory fails to account for GenAI / agentic AI” — they can’t, both predate it (2021, pre-ChatGPT; Hanelt’s corpus ends ~2020). They legitimately support the “valuable existing literature” half, and imply the gap through their pre-GenAI scope and open research agendas — i.e. inferential support, not direct. The next clause bridges to AI explicitly via Holmström (2022), which helps the defense.
