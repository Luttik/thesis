What changed

Most claims in §4.4 rested on a single voice — scale on one quote, costs on one quote, security on one quote. Each was true but thinly evidenced. I added 13 corroborating quotes drawn from the QDPX codes behind this section (Benefit, Drawback, Risk), so every claim now carries breadth across interviewees.

The additions introduce three interviewees not previously quoted in §4.4 (2 · Arjan Dijk, 4 · Dennis Goedegebuure, 8 · Jon Stephan) and add concrete magnitudes (a-year-to-a-week at scale; week-to-two-hours on efficiency). One factual fix: the intro said “five themes” but lists four. Everything is a tracked insertion — nothing existing was deleted except the single word five → four.

Each card: the claim being supported, who already spoke to it, and the quote(s) I added with attribution. Quotes are verbatim from the transcripts (ellipses mark trimmed filler).

4.4.1 Efficiency — a concrete magnitude+1
Claim supported “Efficiency forms the largest and most consistently described benefit … doing more with the same amount of effort.”
Added after that sentence
3Berfun Goodwin · advisor + marketing
…that I would’ve taken, like, a week to put together … within, like, two hours.

Why: the paragraph asserted efficiency is the dominant benefit but jumped straight to Scott Brinker’s caveat that it is not a durable advantage. A concrete week-to-two-hours figure grounds the claim before it is qualified.

4.4.1 Speed — responsiveness, not just throughput+1
Claim supported “Efficiency can also materialize as speed … the ability to run more experiments.”
Added at end of paragraph
4Dennis Goedegebuure · AI advisor — new to §4.4
competition research agents that come back with every change that they made on their landing page, pricing, et cetera, so you can respond faster.

Why: existing quotes framed speed as faster creation. This adds the distinct angle that speed shortens how fast an organisation can react to its market.

4.4.1 Scale — the thinnest claim, now with magnitudes+2
Claim supported “Scale is the benefit most clearly impossible without AI … generating large amounts of content, many variations … automating processes.” Previously rested on a single quote (14 · “armies of super interns”).
Added at end of paragraph
8Jon Stephan · internal AI expert — new to §4.4
[copy that] would take probably about a year [by hand was generated] within… a week [through an AI workflow].
9Lauren Stokowski · internal AI expert (agency)
a human can only think in so many… variations … [the system] can create all these different versions of a campaign.

Why: scale is the benefit the section calls “impossible without AI,” yet it had the least evidence. Two vivid magnitudes (volume and variation) make it the best-supported, not the weakest.

4.4.1 Skillset — the seniority shift+1
Claim supported “Extending the personal skillset … AI enabling work they could not previously do.”
Added at end of paragraph
7Georgio Mosis · AI advisor
I don’t need two of those guys, I need one, a junior, and he can do the same thing.

Why: existing quotes (12, 10) describe individual capability expanding. This adds the organisational corollary the codebook captures — AI lets juniors do senior work, reshaping the seniority mix.

4.4.2 Costs — scale of spend + subsidised prices+2
