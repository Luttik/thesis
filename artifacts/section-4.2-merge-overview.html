Restructuring §4.2 around the manager's rings of control

The reframe Organize §4.2 by who can shape each condition — concentric rings around the marketing manager. Behaviour leads where managers drive (ring 1, their own team); the condition leads where they don't (rings 2–3: other teams, then governance). The ring itself signals the response mode, so the old Leverage / Navigate / Reshape labels are dropped. One condition inside ring 1 carries no behaviour at all — systems / process thinking — and that absence is the finding.

01

The rings model

The marketing manager sits at the centre. Agency falls as you move outward — and so do the verbs. The diagram is the argument: ring 1 is full of moves; ring 3 is something to accept. Systems / process thinking lives in the core, but the manager doesn't see it. Most conditions sit cleanly in one ring; technical resources is the exception — it straddles the ring 1 / ring 2 boundary.

Inward = more control: ring 1 is driven with verbs (behaviour-led). Outward = less control: ring 3 is accepted and worked around (condition-led). Systems / process thinking sits in the core but is under-recognized — the one place inside the team where the verbs stop. Technical resources straddle the boundary: the team owns its data and MarTech (blue chip), but the infrastructure and integration beneath them belong to IT (green).

05

Cross-cutting tensions

These don't pair to a single move — they cut across the rings. Keep them as a short reflective block. all carry over from old §4.2.1

Talent-pipeline paradox

AI deployed on easy junior tasks erodes the junior→senior pipeline — yet juniors adopt AI fastest. "The pyramid on its top." The data offers no resolution.

Personal vs. organizational

Genuinely easy for an individual ("makes life so much easier"), but an unspoken barrier separates personal effectiveness from org-scale effectiveness.

Optics vs. reality

Capability is claimed in pitches before it is operationally present — "say you have something you don't really have, and then build it really quickly."
