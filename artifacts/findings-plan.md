## 1. Storyline (one paragraph)

The Findings chapter answers the central research question — *How do marketing managers create value with agentic AI?* — by reconstructing what participants actually do. The storyline follows the working CGT model in `artifacts/preview.html`: a moving **AI progression** sets the technological backdrop; organisations are pulled into action by **External input** (clients, vendors, markets, regulation); they respond by mobilising three sets of **Internal conditions** (available resources, capacity to change, leadership and governance); these conditions shape **AI adoption** (use cases, sourcing modes, human-AI operating model); and adoption produces **Value outcomes** — efficiency and quality gains alongside sacrifices, risks and possible value erosion. The chapter is descriptive (close to data); interpretation against the literature is held back for the Discussion.

### 4.7 Cross-cutting tensions (synthesis section)
This subchapter synthesises three tensions that recur across blocks and are anchored in the memos:
1. **Hype vs. reality** — participants describe ambitious agentic use cases that, on close reading, are mostly RAG, workflows or scripted automations (memo: *Hype v.s. reality*; *Hard time classifying AI* in `notes.md`).
2. **AI hygiene vs. AI leadership** — most adoption is "doing the proven thing efficiently"; only a few participants describe genuinely differentiating moves (memo: *Getting to outsized returns*).
3. **Friction and the time-lag from potential to adoption** — technical possibility runs far ahead of what tools, organisations and individuals actually use (memo: *Friction with AI*).
4. **Full-stack performance, not just data** — quality of AI depends on data + master prompt + tools + planning + memory + model choice combined (memo: *AI performance*).

### 4.8 Summary: a working answer to the research question
- One-paragraph recap: marketers create value with agentic AI by *iteratively translating a moving technological backdrop into bounded use cases, conditional on the data, skills, change capacity and governance they can mobilise, while trading off efficiency and quality gains against brand, security and adoption risks.*
- Lead-in sentence to the Discussion chapter (no interpretation yet).
