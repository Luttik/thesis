1. Critical content fix

§4.5.3 · Human-AI operating model · CRITICAL factual error

Scott Brinker's three agent types: I had the third one wrong

The draft attributed to P12 a trichotomy of agents for marketers / agents for customers / autonomous agents. Cross-checking P12's quotation file shows the third type is agents of customers — consumer-owned agents acting on the customer's behalf (e.g. ChatGPT or Claude doing deep research, or making an agentic purchase). P12 considered the third category the most disruptive for marketing precisely because it dissolves the firm's traditional control over the customer interface. "Autonomous agents" was my own invention and would have been a misrepresentation of the source.

Before

P12 systematised the operating-model question by distinguishing three kinds of agent: agents for marketers (internal copilots), agents for customers (customer-facing), and autonomous agents (running unattended in the background). The three call for different governance, different measurement and different escalation patterns, and most organisations in the data are operating predominantly in the first category.

After

P12 systematised the operating-model question by distinguishing three kinds of agent: agents for marketers (tools that help marketers with their own work, such as planning and analysis), agents for customers (marketer-controlled agents that interact with customers, such as service or sales-assistant agents on websites), and agents of customers (consumer-owned agents acting on customers' behalf, for example a personal AI doing deep research or making an agentic purchase). P12 considered the third category the most disruptive for marketing because it dissolves the firm's traditional control over the customer interface. Most organisations in the data are operating predominantly in the first category, a handful in the second, and none yet in the third.

3. Verbatim restorations

The draft originally claimed "All quotes are reproduced verbatim from the coded QDA-XML project." On audit, several quotes had silently dropped stuttered repetitions (e.g. "I, I, I work"), trailing fragments (e.g. "Um-…") or self-corrections (e.g. "y- lighthouse"). I have restored the original disfluencies in seven quotes and rewritten the §4.1 statement of how quotes are reproduced so it accurately describes the practice.

7. Verification approach

  1. Building a small Python helper that searches each participant's qdpx-coding/quotations/*.md file for the anchor phrases I used in the chapter (e.g. "hockey stick", "Swiss army", "agents of customers").
