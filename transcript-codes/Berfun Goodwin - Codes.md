## Participant Profile

Berfun Goodwin leads the digital marketing strategy and analytics team at MilliporeSigma (Merck). Her team of 11 functions as the "innovation arm" of digital marketing, testing new channels and tactics before scaling them across the organization. She has a background in industrial engineering (bachelor's) and international marketing (MBA), with ~10 years in digital roles spanning automotive (Turkey), retail analytics (Ahold/Albert Heijn), insurance, ecommerce, and now life sciences. She self-identifies as coming from an analytics background and is comfortable with SQL.

## 1. AI Applications & Use Cases

### 1.1 Complex Analytics
**Code:** `AI-APPLICATION:ANALYTICS`

Berfun uses AI (Claude 4.5 Sonnet via internal GPT instance) for complex marketing analytics: dumping data, asking questions, and extracting insights she wouldn't have found manually.

> "Agentic AI, I use to answer complex, like, analytics questions. [...] I dump a bunch of data and then give it more, like, has to go through some steps to answer my question." (line 112)

> "When I ask, like, what else do you see in the data? Beyond, like, what I asked, It gives me, like, interesting points where I then [...] enables me to ask the next level of questions." (line 151)

### 1.2 AI-Generated Product Imagery
**Code:** `AI-APPLICATION:PRODUCT-IMAGERY`

A major initiative to generate product images for the website using AI to fill gaps in product photography coverage, which was blocking ecommerce scaling.

> "We were, like, really dependent on, like, someone actually taking, you know, product pictures and uploading them on the website. And not all products had coverage. And we are trying to scale our ecommerce business, and you can't scale without image." (line 241)

### 1.3 Content Creation (with Human Approval)
**Code:** `AI-APPLICATION:CONTENT-CREATION`

AI-generated marketing copy, but always requiring human review and approval due to regulatory and compliance requirements.

> "AI generated content, So still content isn't, like, somebody will have to like, a human will have to touch an approve it afterwards because if all the regulations and stuff like that." (line 133)

### 1.4 Dynamic Creative Optimization (DCO)
**Code:** `AI-APPLICATION:DCO`

Previously tested (3-4 years ago): an AI system that tests many combinations of creative elements (imagery, copy, CTA) to find the highest-performing combination.

> "You put in all those different components and variations and it creates [...] the marketing asset in a way that's gonna create the most click throughs [...] It learns along the way, like, tests different combinations and finds the right combination." (line 139)

### 1.5 Brand Guidelines Assistant
**Code:** `AI-APPLICATION:BRAND-ASSISTANT`

A custom assistant trained on branding guidelines and personas that evaluates whether content is brand-aligned and provides guidance for addressing specific personas.

> "The branding team [...] trained the assistant on our different on our branding guidelines and different personas. And then now that assistant tells you if this is a pro brand aligned, or, like, tells you, like, this is what you should do to address this type of persona." (line 171)

### 1.6 Synthesia Avatars
**Code:** `AI-APPLICATION:AVATAR-VIDEO`

Using Synthesia to create AI avatar videos for team meeting recaps and training materials. Replaces text-based recap emails with more engaging video summaries.

> "We started sending along [...] a more engaged version of it with the Synthesia video. So, like, head of our team has an avatar and [...] the recap. [...] more people will watch a, like, two minute video than read the entire email." (lines 271, 279)

### 1.7 SQL Code Enhancement
**Code:** `AI-APPLICATION:CODE-IMPROVEMENT`

Berfun uses AI to improve her SQL queries, adding complexity and dimensions she wouldn't manage on her own.

> "I write a code, a basic code, and then tell it to say, like, I wanna modify it in this way. I wanna like, add this dimension, that dimension, and, like, it improves my code." (line 319)

### 1.8 Pricing/Promotion Agent
**Code:** `AI-APPLICATION:PRICING-AGENT`

A custom agent that analyzes pricing datasets and recommends products for promotion based on discount sensitivity patterns.

> "I created one agent. That says, like, that digests an a dataset about pricing and tells me use like, these products to run a promotion because there's opportunity. To, like, have discounts." (line 349)

### 1.9 Tableau Einstein
**Code:** `AI-APPLICATION:BI-INTEGRATION`

Tableau's AI feature (Einstein) that allows asking natural-language questions of dashboards and getting more sophisticated answers than standard reporting.

> "Tableau, has Einstein I think, where you can, like, ask questions and, like, it gives you more, like, sophisticated answers and analyses and also like, next level to, like, a regular reporting." (line 129)

## 5. Organizational Obstacles

### 5.1 Human Resistance
**Code:** `OBSTACLE:HUMAN-RESISTANCE`

Pockets of resistance exist, particularly from groups that are less digitally-oriented or afraid of becoming irrelevant. The company is ~100 years old and some groups are not technology-forward.

> "There are some, like, groups that are not as technology forward and or afraid that they will be irrelevant So there's some resistance in terms of, like, us using AI." (line 231)

### 5.2 Content Approval Friction
**Code:** `OBSTACLE:APPROVAL-PROCESS`

The long organizational content approval process creates friction when AI can produce content much faster than it can be approved.

> "There's, like, a long, like, approval process. In the organization to get, like, content approved and written [...] this streamlines all of it, so that is creating some friction." (line 231)

### 5.3 Data Size Limitations
**Code:** `OBSTACLE:DATA-LIMITATIONS`

The internal GPT tool can only handle ~30MB of data, preventing analysis of larger datasets. Not yet connected to data lakes.

> "The RGPT instance [...] can only digest so much data. So if I try to dump anything over, like, 30 MBs, it doesn't it doesn't do it. So sometimes I wanna work with, like, larger datasets." (line 315)

### 5.4 Business Context Gap
**Code:** `OBSTACLE:CONTEXT-GAP`

AI tools lack business context about data dimensions, requiring manual explanation each time data is uploaded.

> "I still have to provide, like, more business context to the dimensions that I upload." (line 343)

### 5.5 Leadership Understanding Gap
**Code:** `OBSTACLE:LEADERSHIP-UNDERSTANDING`

While leadership encourages AI use, they don't always understand what they are asking for.

> "They don't know what they're talking about most of the time, but, like, they're like, use more AI." (line 231)

## 6. Agentic vs. Generative Distinction

**Code:** `CONCEPT:AGENTIC-VS-GENERATIVE`

Berfun recognizes a clear distinction between generative AI (content creation, writing improvement) and agentic AI (complex analytics with multi-step reasoning). She categorizes most of her team's current work as generative, with analytics use cases being more agentic.

> "I think we're using more, like, generative AI. I think agentic AI, I use to answer complex, like, analytics questions." (lines 110–112)

Her definition of agentic:

> "Agency AI is more sophisticated; it has its own brain. You can give it more, like, sophisticated questions, and it's going to, like, break it down to smaller tasks for you. And then and then give you an answer." (line 101)

## Key Takeaways for Thesis

1. **Analytics is a high-value AI use case:** The biggest perceived value comes from AI-assisted analytics (speed, depth, accuracy), not just content generation. This aligns with the thesis focus on applications beyond content.
2. **Generative before agentic:** The organization is primarily in a "generative AI" phase, with agentic applications emerging in analytics. This suggests a maturity progression.
3. **Self-service agent building is powerful:** Enabling marketers to build their own agents (pricing analysis, brand guidelines) without engineering support dramatically lowers the barrier to adoption.
4. **Trust through governance:** Company-sanctioned tools with compliance frameworks build user confidence, enabling faster adoption than a "bring your own tool" approach.
5. **Measurement gap:** Despite leadership enthusiasm, there are no consistent KPIs for AI impact. This could become a barrier to sustained investment.
6. **The "marketer as data engineer":** AI empowers marketing professionals to perform tasks (SQL, data analysis) that previously required specialized technical roles, blurring the boundary between marketing and data engineering.
7. **Approval processes create friction:** The speed of AI content generation collides with traditional content approval workflows, creating organizational friction.
8. **Infrastructure is the next bottleneck:** Data size limitations and lack of connection to data lakes are the main technical obstacles, not the AI models themselves.
