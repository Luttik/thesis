# Transcript Coding: Rolf Mulder

**Interview Date:** Feb 19  
**Participant Role:** Co-founder, Instant Crush (AI-powered marketing focus tool for mid-market)  
**Organization:** Instant Crush — startup (went live May 2025, already pivoted once)  
**Interview Duration:** ~45 minutes  

---

## Participant Profile

Rolf Mulder is co-founder of Instant Crush, a startup building "the ultimate focus tool for marketing departments." The product translates data into insights, direction, and actionable roadmaps for mid-market companies. Rolf handles the commercial side, fundraising, and strategy while his co-founder is the systems thinker and product/technical lead. Rolf graduated in traditional marketing before the SaaS explosion and has nearly 20 years of marketing experience. He has a strong marketing science orientation (Binet & Field, Byron Sharp) and is vocal about the over-indexing on efficiency and the under-investment in brand building. He is based in Rotterdam, Netherlands. His perspective is that of a marketing strategist and founder who sees AI as a tool for marketing effectiveness, not just efficiency.

## 1. AI Applications & Use Cases

### 1.1 AI-Powered Marketing Analytics & Advisory Platform
**Code:** `AI-APPLICATION:MARKETING-ADVISORY-PLATFORM`

Instant Crush provides AI-driven competitive analysis, multi-card customer journey simulation, strategic recommendations, and marketing roadmaps. The platform tells mid-market companies what to do, what not to do, and why — going beyond dashboards to context and direction.

> "We promise very very tight focus and and and understanding of where you are at with your marketing. And translate it to a road map." (line 131)

> "We present facts and and and context. [...] It's not about the dashboard. It's about the message it has and how you convey that message." (line 133)

### 1.2 AI as Sparring Partner (Not Executor)
**Code:** `AI-APPLICATION:SPARRING-PARTNER`

Rolf uses AI as a thinking partner — drafting ideas, identifying gaps in reasoning, proposing how to fill those gaps — but explicitly not for autonomous execution. They stepped back from letting AI create final outputs because quality was insufficient.

> "I gather my thoughts, I can make a quick draft, ask AI to identify the gaps in my thinking, come back with a proposal how to fill in those gaps, and then as sort more of a sparring partner." (line 51)

> "What we really, really stepped away from is that AI makes something for us without human intervention." (line 53)

### 1.3 Automated Bottom-Funnel Content at Scale
**Code:** `AI-APPLICATION:BOTTOM-FUNNEL-CONTENT`

An earlier proof-of-concept created ~1,000 fully automated web pages for a recruitment organization. This worked because the content was standardized and bottom-funnel. The insight: automated content works for sales activation but fails for creative, on-brand, emotional content.

> "We created content for a recruitment organization [...] we made some sort of a hub for them. But everything there was fully automated. So we created something about thousand web pages fully automated. But that in that use case, it worked. Because it was very standardized." (line 71)

### 1.4 Competitive Analysis at Scale
**Code:** `AI-APPLICATION:COMPETITIVE-ANALYSIS`

AI replaces consultant work of plowing through competitors and content. The platform benchmarks brand performance against competitors using public signals.

> "The other one is to do analysis at scale. For customers. So that is normally a consultant has to plow through a lot of competitors a lot of content, and now AI can do most of that work." (line 51)

### 1.5 Customer's Deal Notification Agent
**Code:** `AI-APPLICATION:DEAL-NOTIFICATION`

A customer of Instant Crush uses agentic AI to proactively inform their own customers of good deals at the moment they arise.

> "He uses agentic AI to inform his customers of a good possible deal at the time the deal arises." (line 61)

### 1.6 Living Web Content (Experimental)
**Code:** `AI-APPLICATION:LIVING-CONTENT`

Experimenting with web content (blog posts) that is "alive" — it self-enriches daily by scraping new data, staying perpetually current.

> "A sort of a blog post or a piece of content is sort of alive. So it keeps becoming enriched and rich every day. But it's still scraping in data." (line 63)

## 2. Benefits

### 2.1 Strategic Focus and Direction
**Code:** `BENEFIT:STRATEGIC-FOCUS`

The primary value is not efficiency but focus: helping marketers know what to work on, what to prioritize, and what to stop doing. Reduces the anxiety of "am I doing the right things?"

> "They find it very relaxing that they don't have to think about what should I do tomorrow? Am I doing the right things?" (line 173)

> "Not only what to do, but also what not to do because that's where we also win a lot of bounce points." (line 211)

### 2.2 Management Understanding / Actionable Insights
**Code:** `BENEFIT:MANAGEMENT-UNDERSTANDING`

AI translates data into context and direction rather than just presenting dashboards. Senior management doesn't understand dashboards; they understand actionable recommendations with context.

> "A dashboard doesn't give any context. [...] Most of the time a dashboard tells that things are not broken or not going down, but it never tells you what to do next." (line 197)

### 2.3 Automation Bias as Advantage
**Code:** `BENEFIT:AUTOMATION-BIAS`

When AI delivers the same recommendation a consultant would, management accepts it more readily. The machine is perceived as more truthful than the person.

> "When I, as a consultant, told the same things to a company, it was always sort of I had to defend my position. But now I say the same thing but with my machine. And then it's the truth. [...] People are very susceptible to automation bias." (lines 169–171)

### 2.4 Consultant-Quality at Subscription Price
**Code:** `BENEFIT:CONSULTANT-REPLACEMENT`

AI enables consultant-grade analysis (previously €120/hour) to be delivered as a subscription, making strategic marketing support accessible to mid-market companies.

> "I can have AI do things that I used to do as a consultant, but then for €120 an hour, I can now it as a subscription." (line 141)

### 2.5 Effectiveness Over Efficiency
**Code:** `BENEFIT:EFFECTIVENESS`

Rolf explicitly argues that marketing's problem is not efficiency but effectiveness. If AI helps companies do better marketing (not just cheaper marketing), they should invest more, not less.

> "Marketing does not have an efficiency problem. [...] Marketing has an effectiveness problem because there's a lot of data [...] the quality of marketing that's being produced has been dropping for about twenty years." (lines 143–147)

> "If instant crush fulfills its promise people will spend more on marketing and not less because they see it works." (line 157)

## 3. Drawbacks & Risks

### 3.1 Inconsistency in AI Outputs
**Code:** `RISK:OUTPUT-INCONSISTENCY`

Same analysis run multiple times produces slightly different results (e.g., ranked 7th vs. 8th). Not catastrophic but creates distrust.

> "We run a prompt three times, Sometimes it's you're in eighth place, and sometimes you're in seventh place. [...] There's a little bit of inconsistency there." (line 235)

### 3.2 Automation Bias (Negative Side)
**Code:** `RISK:AUTOMATION-BIAS`

The same automation bias that helps adoption also creates risk: people accept AI recommendations uncritically, including potential hallucinations.

> "People are very susceptible to automation bias." (line 171)

### 3.3 Prompt Engineering is Extremely Time-Consuming
**Code:** `RISK:PROMPT-ENGINEERING-COST`

Building production-quality prompts and agents takes months, not days. Prompts are 3-4 pages long. Once built they scale well, but the upfront investment is massive.

> "Creating really good AI prompts or agents takes a long long time. [...] It's once you have it, it scales incredibly. [...] But an agent that can do a certain task [...] that takes a long time." (lines 245–247)

### 3.4 AI Overpromised / Hype
**Code:** `RISK:AI-HYPE`

AI is systematically oversold. Customers expect a one-button solution. Agencies over-promise on AI capabilities they don't actually have.

> "The big thing is hype. So AI is being overpromised all the time. [...] Especially agencies they have to adopt but they don't have the skills yet. So they over promise on almost everything." (lines 251–255)

### 3.5 AI Falls Short for Brand/Creative Content
**Code:** `RISK:CREATIVE-LIMITATION`

AI-generated content works for standardized bottom-funnel material but fails for creative, emotional, on-brand content. The more creativity and brand voice required, the less AI can autonomously produce.

> "What we see within marketing is once you have to have more creativity in the output and also if it needs to be on brand that AI still falls very very short. [...] It seems to only be very useful for very bottom funnel sales activation content." (line 71)

## 4. Organizational Enablers

### 4.1 Narrow Solution Focus
**Code:** `ENABLER:NARROW-FOCUS`

The most important lesson: narrow down what the AI solution does. Being too broad leads to disappointment. Specificity builds trust and delivers measurable value.

> "You really have to be specific about what you deliver and what you solve. [...] It's much more important with AI to narrow down your place within AI as a solution." (line 255)

### 4.2 Proprietary Data
**Code:** `ENABLER:PROPRIETARY-DATA`

Unique data creates a competitive moat for AI solutions. AI can also help generate proprietary data through scaled conversations and structured information collection.

> "Proprietary data so data you have unique to yours. [...] That's a big opportunity." (line 257)

### 4.3 Expectation Management
**Code:** `ENABLER:EXPECTATION-MANAGEMENT`

Actively managing what AI will and won't deliver is essential. The gap between AI hype and reality is the biggest obstacle to adoption success.

> "We have to reduce expectations sometimes. We have to be very very clear what we [...] When we started, we were too broad. In our offering, and that also gave the expectation that AI is some sort of one button solution." (lines 251–253)

### 4.4 Marketing Science Literacy
**Code:** `ENABLER:MARKETING-SCIENCE`

Understanding how marketing actually works (brand building, the 60/40 split, mental availability) is prerequisite for applying AI meaningfully. Without it, AI just accelerates bad marketing.

> "Most people don't know how marketing works [...] only less than 30% of people who are working in marketing have studied marketing." (line 81)

## 5. Organizational Obstacles

### 5.1 AI Hype / Overpromising
**Code:** `OBSTACLE:AI-HYPE`

The biggest obstacle is the gap between what AI is promised to do and what it can actually deliver. This creates distrust when reality falls short of expectation.

> "Most AI solutions we see in the marketing side, disregard most marketing science." (line 75)

### 5.2 Marketing Data Illiteracy
**Code:** `OBSTACLE:DATA-ILLITERACY`

Marketers don't understand data; data people don't understand marketing. Bridging this gap is one of the hardest organizational challenges.

> "Marketers are so data illiterate [...] data people then again sometimes don't really understand how marketing works. And to bring that together, that's [hard]." (line 307)

### 5.3 SaaS Platform Lock-In / Slow Innovation
**Code:** `OBSTACLE:PLATFORM-LOCK-IN`

Existing MarTech platforms (HubSpot, etc.) innovate too slowly with AI and have too much lock-in. AI is "slapped on" as a feature rather than fundamentally rethinking the product.

> "I think Hubspot's maybe their pace of innovation is too slow. They have there's a lot of lock in. [...] Everybody slaps on some AI in their offering these days, especially old SaaS businesses." (line 121)

### 5.4 Technical Talent Shortage
**Code:** `OBSTACLE:TALENT-SHORTAGE`

People who understand both marketing and AI/data are extremely rare. The talent pool for AI-literate marketing analytics is very small.

> "The talent pool in analytics is so small of people who can apply AI, understand AI, understand are data literate." (line 307)

### 5.5 Developer Resistance to AI-First Working
**Code:** `OBSTACLE:DEVELOPER-RESISTANCE`

Even developers resist adopting AI-first workflows. A developer working for Instant Crush could not transform his way of working away from manual coding.

> "People are still not adapting to AI first working. We had a developer working for us. He could not translate transform his way of working." (line 339)

## 6. Conceptual Codes

### 6.1 "Faster Horse" Problem
**Code:** `CONCEPT:FASTER-HORSE`

Most AI solutions optimize existing processes rather than rethinking marketing fundamentally. Rolf explicitly names this: "purely efficiency and that's not the biggest bottleneck."

> "Most AI solutions are really based are made built for building the faster horse as you might know. Purely efficiency." (line 39)

### 6.2 Marketing Has an Effectiveness Problem, Not an Efficiency Problem
**Code:** `CONCEPT:EFFECTIVENESS-VS-EFFICIENCY`

The central thesis of Rolf's worldview: marketing's quality has been dropping for 20 years because non-specialists took over. AI making bad marketing cheaper doesn't help. AI making marketing better — that's transformative.

> "Efficiency increases, but effectiveness of marketing decreases. So the quality of marketing that's being produced has been dropping for about twenty years because it's now [non-]professionals within organizations." (line 147)

### 6.3 Brand Building vs. Sales Activation (60/40)
**Code:** `CONCEPT:BRAND-VS-ACTIVATION`

Marketing science says 60% brand building (emotional, broad reach, long-term) and 40% sales activation (rational, targeted, short-term). The SaaS era pushed everything toward activation. AI is most applicable to the 40% activation but the 60% is where the real value lies.

> "Only 5% of consumers in every shape or form in every industry are in the market. So your efforts towards those 5%, I think, those you can do really well with AI. [...] But the brand building stuff for the 95% of the consumers who are not yet in the market. [...] AI can definitely help reduce cost in creation. But not so much in the thinking." (line 115)

### 6.4 Vanity Metrics as the Enemy
**Code:** `CONCEPT:VANITY-METRICS`

ROAS, LinkedIn views, SEO traffic — these are often vanity metrics that don't drive real growth. ROAS can be deceptive: "a lot of people were already going to buy." AI solutions that optimize for vanity metrics perpetuate bad marketing.

> "Vanity metrics is actually what we're battling against." (line 191)

> "ROAS is deceptive in a way." (line 195)

### 6.5 Accelerated Intelligence > Artificial Intelligence
**Code:** `CONCEPT:ACCELERATED-INTELLIGENCE`

Rolf reframes AI as "accelerated intelligence" rather than "artificial intelligence" — the technology amplifies human thinking rather than replacing it.

> "It's sometimes more accelerated intelligence than artificial intelligence." (line 45)

### 6.6 Technoplasmosis
**Code:** `CONCEPT:TECHNOPLASMOSIS`

A concept Rolf references (promising to share the article) about how technology infiltrates and reshapes organizational processes in ways that don't necessarily improve outcomes.

> "I'll send you an article. It's called technoplasmosis." (line 101)

## 7. Agentic vs. Generative Distinction

**Code:** `CONCEPT:AGENTIC-VS-GENERATIVE`

Rolf defines agentic AI as proactive — continuously doing things without being asked, within constraints. Generative AI is prompt-based: you ask, it responds. His product leans agentic on the analytics side (continuous monitoring, proactive recommendations) but deliberately not on the content side.

> "Agentic AI means that AI is proactive. [...] Something that continuously does something without you asking within certain constraints." (line 47)

---

## 8. The Pivot Story

**Code:** `CONCEPT:PIVOT-FROM-AUTOMATION`

Instant Crush pivoted from fully automated content creation (which worked for standardized bottom-funnel content at scale) to AI-assisted strategy and analytics. The pivot was driven by the realization that autonomous AI content doesn't scale beyond narrow use cases because it lacks creativity and brand understanding.

> "What we really, really stepped away from is that AI makes something for us without human intervention. [...] We actually see that when you go beyond one use case, you have to have lots of human intervention for achieving a certain quality. So everything everything but the making, we do with AI." (line 53)

## Key Takeaways for Thesis

1. **Effectiveness > Efficiency:** Rolf provides the strongest counter-narrative to the efficiency-first framing seen in other interviews. Marketing's problem is doing the wrong things well, not doing the right things slowly. AI that makes bad marketing cheaper is not valuable.
2. **The pivot story is a cautionary tale:** Instant Crush moved from fully automated content to human-AI collaboration after finding that autonomous content doesn't scale beyond standardized bottom-funnel use cases. This directly informs the thesis question about where agentic AI creates value vs. where it falls short.
3. **Focus and direction as primary value:** Not speed, not cost reduction — the most valued outcome is knowing what to do next. This is a fundamentally different value proposition than any other interviewee has articulated.
4. **Automation bias is double-edged:** The same cognitive bias that makes AI recommendations more persuasive than human ones also creates risk when those recommendations are wrong.
5. **Brand building is AI-resistant:** The 60% of marketing that drives long-term growth (brand building, emotional messaging, mental availability) is precisely where AI falls shortest. AI is most applicable to the 40% sales activation layer.
6. **Marketing science illiteracy compounds AI risk:** Without understanding how marketing actually works, AI just automates and scales bad practices faster.
7. **Narrow focus is the enabler:** The most important success factor is ruthlessly narrowing what the AI solution does. Breadth leads to disappointment.
8. **First startup/founder perspective:** Rolf brings the first founder-operator view to the sample — resource-constrained, pivot-experienced, investor-facing, and deeply skeptical of the efficiency narrative.
